# Paper candle coverage — Hyperliquid native evidence

**Investigated:** October 20, 2026, 19:25–19:30 UTC.

**Scope:** Opus Finding 2 (conditional P1), not an integration sign-off.

## Finding

**The sampled native API emits zero-trade candles; it does not omit those
minutes.** Two quiet primary perpetuals each returned all 180 requested closed
one-minute slots, including **343 explicit zero-trade candles across the two
markets**. No interior or boundary holes were observed. One bounded repeat was
byte-identical. This contradicts routine zero-trade omission **in these samples**;
it does not establish a universal completeness or finality guarantee. [E]

Consequently, the premise that an ordinary quiet minute inevitably triggers
F2's missing-candle path is **not established**. The report's synthetic
missing-input result remains evidence about that path, not proof of native
venue omissions. Do not promote its conditional P1 to a proven market-data
incident, or implement its suggested absent-minute skipping without additional
coverage evidence. [L1, L2, E]

## What the primary sources establish

- The official REST candle section documents the request window in epoch
  milliseconds, supports `1m` and `5m`, and limits availability to the most recent
  5,000 candles. Its example is a candle array, not a coverage/completeness
  envelope. The inspected section provides no zero-trade omission rule or
  settlement/finality guarantee. [S1]
- The official candle schema identifies `n` as the trade count, `v` as base-unit
  volume and `t`/`T` as open/close milliseconds. The WebSocket schema corroborates
  field meanings, not the REST representation of prices. [S2]
- The official Python SDK's `candles_snapshot` forwards the market/window
  request and returns the POST result. It supplies no completeness validation
  or extra omission guarantee. Inspected at immutable commit
  `7ee976d123b1e04295e4a1e37a424ca6a13bef88`, lines 475–505. [S3]

The repository's chart-cache statement that missing slots can be legitimate
no-trade periods is a local chart policy, **not primary-source evidence of
Hyperliquid behavior**. HTTP 200, neighboring candles, matching prices, or a
short response alone do not prove that an absent slot contains no trades. [L3;
inference from S1–S3]

### Cross-checks and their limits

- Across all 72 five-minute buckets, trade counts and exact decimal volumes
  matched the sums of their one-minute children. For buckets containing trades,
  aggregating OHLC from the **nonzero-trade** children also matched. Naively
  including carried zero-trade prices produced five OHLC mismatches for TNSR
  and two for HMSTR. This is evidence against treating carried prices as fresh
  trade observations, not evidence of a missing minute. [E]
- TNSR's recent-trades request returned ten rows spanning
  16:23:34.372–19:18:49.709 UTC; nine were inside the sampled window, all in
  candles reporting positive `n`. This bounded recent list does **not**
  establish complete historical trade coverage. Absence from it proves no
  empty segment. No completeness/pagination contract for that read was
  established by this investigation. [E]
- The repeat at 19:27:55 UTC had the same raw SHA-256 as the initial TNSR
  one-minute response. Stability over one repeat is corroboration, not
  immutable finality. No archive-boundary, outage, late-correction or
  genuinely missing-response case was observed. [E]

## Minimal corrective contract — recommendation, not implementation

1. **Distinguish reported empty from unknown.** A validated, sufficiently
   settled native candle with `n = 0` and zero volume affirmatively reports
   no trades for its interval. An absent candle, missing/invalid count,
   contradictory count/volume, failed read or potentially truncated window
   does not. Do not default unknown counts to zero.
2. **Retain the evidence needed by paper evaluation.** The inspected
   Hyperliquid adapter validates and retains `n` as `VenueCandle.Trades`;
   `PaperCandle` retains only timestamps/OHLC. Its touch predicate therefore
   cannot distinguish a zero-trade carry from a trade candle. For a
   trade-observation model, preserve the count and advance through reported
   empty minutes **without entry/exit touches**, preserving the previous
   observed trade close for a later covered jump. Never fabricate a flat
   candle or execution to fill an absent slot. This is a separate contract
   implication of the observed `n = 0` rows, not proof of F2's omission
   premise. [L2, L4, E]
3. **Treat an actual hole as unresolved coverage.** Validate the requested
   grid explicitly; keep the checkpoint before the first unknown interval.
   Adjacent candles, HTTP 200 or a matching five-minute total are insufficient
   to skip that interval. A nonempty five-minute candle cannot establish
   which absent minute traded. No new five-minute fallback is needed for
   these fully populated samples.
4. **Use bounded recovery, not an optimistic coverage declaration.** Read
   behind a settlement lag and permit a finite retry/deadline for transient
   incompleteness before recording an unrecoverable gap. Neither a chosen
   lag nor repeated identical holes proves no trades. Retention loss,
   truncation or an exhausted recovery budget stays explicit unknown,
   unscored and subject to existing account-chronology/capital safeguards.
   The parent owns scheduler/lag implementation.
5. **Require affirmative evidence before advancing an absent segment.**
   Examples would be a source completeness guarantee plus a documented
   sparse-minute rule, or complete trade/explicit zero-trade evidence for
   that segment. None was obtained for an actually absent minute here.
   If future evidence justifies an empty-segment representation, advance
   with no touches, and never bridge a price jump over unknown coverage.

Useful regression cases are native zero-trade rows with a level exactly at the
carried price; a missing interior minute despite HTTP 200; an omitted `n`;
retention/truncation; bounded retry recovery; and a later trade after a known
empty run versus after an unknown gap. These are recommendations only; no
builds or application tests were run.

**Confidence:** high for the recorded behavior of these two markets/windows
and the inspected field schema; insufficient to guarantee all native windows,
prove sparse omissions never occur, measure settlement lag, or classify any
unobserved hole as no trades. There is no authority here for live execution,
deployment, migration or release.
